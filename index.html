Can these garment measurements be compared?

Two readable numbers are not necessarily comparable. Before calculating a difference, establish what was measured and how. This small checklist organizes your own checks; it cannot inspect a chart or confirm a seller's statement.

Equal numbers can answer different questions

All values below are invented for explanation. They do not describe an order, a product test or a seller's current chart.

54 cm shoulder-to-cuff versus 54 cm neck-to-cuffThese sleeve measurements have different starting points. The equal number does not make the methods equivalent.
A 54 cm flat garment width versus a 108 cm body circumferenceThe objects and methods differ. Doubling a width does not establish a suitable body size or wearing ease.
22 inches versus 54 cm, with matching flat-width methodsRecord the conversion first: 22 × 2.54 = 55.88 cm. The arithmetic difference is +1.88 cm, subject to the original measurement's uncertainty.
54 cm from one model's chart versus a selected option whose model is unclearConfirm the option-to-model mapping. A chart cannot silently be applied to every design in a collage.

A completed checklist is a starting point

If all five conditions are supported, you can calculate candidate minus reference for that one dimension. A positive result means a larger recorded dimension, not a recommendation to choose a size. Cut, seam position, stretch, intended layering and measurement uncertainty can still matter.

A seller's stated tolerance is a separate source statement. Save its wording and context rather than treating a small difference as automatically acceptable. When an endpoint is hidden in a ruler photograph, ask for a clearer view; a tolerance does not repair unreadable evidence.
